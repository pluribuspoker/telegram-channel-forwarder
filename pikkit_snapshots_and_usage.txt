Pikkit NFL snapshots and usage plan

Status

Approved design implemented locally. The worksheet, timers, and services have
not been created or deployed.


Goal

Capture time-series Pikkit community betting splits for every NFL game in a
dedicated Google Sheet, then expose a single as-of reader that dashboards,
expert inputs, aggregators, grading analysis, and future research can reuse.


Settled decisions

1. Store snapshots in a new append-only Google Sheet tab named
   nfl_pikkit_snapshots.
2. Capture each available upcoming NFL game at least once every 12 hours.
3. Capture one mandatory final snapshot two hours before kickoff.
4. Never fetch a postgame value as a substitute for a missed pregame snapshot.
   Pikkit returns 403 for completed games, so missing historical observations
   remain missing.
5. Match games using the exact away team, home team, and kickoff date. A
   one-team match is not sufficient.
6. Retain the Pikkit event id after discovery and use it for later snapshots.
7. Keep Pikkit splits independent from BetOnline lines. Pikkit supplies
   market-side percentages but does not identify the spread or total line to
   which those percentages correspond. Consumers may time-join these snapshots
   with nfl_line_snapshots without claiming that the two sources share a line.
8. All historical consumers must use the latest snapshot at or before their
   as-of time. Later snapshots must never leak into an earlier opinion or
   analysis.
9. The first downstream consumer will be a dedicated Pikkit Expert voice.
10. The Pikkit Expert will launch in shadow mode: its opinions are generated,
    automatically approved after validation, displayed, and graded, but do not
    enter either God Expert pool. Shadow mode remains active until an explicit
    evidence threshold and activation decision are approved.
11. Generate two Pikkit Expert opinions per game:
    - an initial opinion immediately after the first successful snapshot;
    - a final opinion immediately after the mandatory T-2h snapshot.
12. The initial opinion records which observable Pikkit movements would
    strengthen, weaken, or invalidate its read. The final opinion evaluates
    those pre-registered watch conditions against the actual snapshot history.
13. Both phases use Claude Opus 4.8 at max effort.
14. The initial opinion is forced to PASS on both side and total. The final
    T-2h opinion may issue side and/or total shadow legs under its registered
    policy so those legs can be evaluated without affecting God.
15. The Pikkit Expert remains in shadow mode until the operator explicitly
    approves activation. No sample count, score, or automated threshold may
    activate it.
16. The T-2h snapshot is the canonical final snapshot. Once it succeeds, no
    later baseline or replacement capture is scheduled for that game.
17. Shadow generation has no arbitrary minimum Pikkit volume. Generate when at
    least one complete market is available, preserve the counts, and treat low
    volume as a no-signal warning and later evaluation dimension.
18. Missing or invalid data blocks a shadow leg only for the affected market.
    It does not suppress an otherwise valid game opinion.
19. Snapshot collection and Opus generation run as separate services. The
    collector never calls a model; an idempotent generation runner discovers
    committed snapshots that still need an initial or final opinion.
20. Desk presentation stays compact. Picks and Offline show one latest Pikkit
    line; the DM detail view shows the complete initial watch and final
    evaluation side by side.
21. The Pikkit Expert is a predictive shadow voice. Its deterministic baseline
    is the contemporaneous BetOnline market; Opus may adjust that baseline
    using the Pikkit splits, estimated sportsbook exposure, time-series
    movement, and supplied historical calibration.
22. Code, not the prompt, calculates all percentages, implied probabilities,
    normalized sportsbook-net scenarios, movement, buckets, and historical
    records. The prompt interprets only the validated deterministic package.
23. Sportsbook-benefit calculations cover moneyline, spread, and total. They
    report both the lower-handle outcome proxy and a BetOnline-priced normalized
    net estimate.
24. The exposure estimate explicitly assumes BetOnline has the same side-level
    handle distribution as Pikkit and that the representative BetOnline terms
    apply to that handle. It is an analytical estimate, not a claim about
    BetOnline's actual private liability.


Current source

pikkit.py already provides the required API access:

- fetch_events_for_date(date) discovers Pikkit events through /events/all.
- fetch_splits(pikkit_event_id) reads /event/foryou/{id}.
- NFL is already mapped to the Pikkit NFL league.
- The response contains moneyline, spread, and total breakdowns.
- Each side contains bet_pct, handle_pct, label, and bets.
- The event-level response also contains num_picks and total_wagered.

The existing tracker path calls get_pick_splits(), reduces the full event
response to one parsed pick's side, and stores it in parse_cache.json.
angles/extract_angles.py reads that cached projection. NFL snapshot collection
must instead preserve the complete event breakdown.

Audit and forward compatibility

- source                         pikkit
- source_json                    canonical normalized splits object

Percentages are stored as decimal fractions in the 0..1 range. Bet counts and
num_picks are integers; total_wagered is a non-negative finite number. Missing
markets remain blank rather than being converted to zero.


Scheduling and idempotency

Run a lightweight collector frequently enough to hit the T-2h boundary
reliably; the collector decides which games are due rather than fetching every
game on every invocation.

For each upcoming game:

1. Discover and bind its Pikkit event using both teams and kickoff date.
2. If no successful baseline snapshot exists in the previous 12 hours, capture
   a baseline snapshot.
3. When the game enters the two-hour pregame boundary, capture exactly one
   final_t_minus_2h snapshot.
4. After the final snapshot succeeds, stop scheduling captures for that game.
   Otherwise stop at kickoff and report the missing final observation.

Use a deterministic snapshot_id based on the NFL event id and scheduled capture
identity. Retries for the same scheduled capture therefore cannot append a
duplicate row. A failed request appends no data row and remains eligible for a
bounded retry before kickoff.

The final capture should target kickoff minus two hours, with a small scheduler
tolerance so timer jitter does not miss it. Its scheduled_for_utc remains the
exact T-2h timestamp even when captured a few minutes later.


Validation

Before appending a row:

- Require the NFL and Pikkit away/home teams to match as an unordered game pair
  with the same home/away orientation.
- Require the kickoff dates to agree in Eastern time.
- Require the Pikkit event to be not_started or otherwise explicitly pregame.
- Require every present percentage to be finite and within 0..1.
- Require opposing bet percentages and handle percentages to sum to
  approximately 1.0 when both sides are present.
- Require non-negative integer bet counts, num_picks, and total_wagered.
- Canonicalize source_json and verify payload_sha256 before returning rows to a
  consumer.


Reader contract

Add one shared reader rather than letting each consumer query the worksheet
differently. It should support:

- all snapshots for an NFL event;
- latest snapshot;
- latest snapshot at or before an as-of timestamp;
- first available snapshot;
- final T-2h snapshot;
- movement between two snapshots.

The reader must return both raw stored fields and deterministic derived
features. It must not silently substitute a future snapshot when no valid
as-of row exists.


Candidate derived features

For each market side:

- bet_pct
- handle_pct
- handle_minus_bet_pct
- bet-count share
- change in bet_pct since the first snapshot
- change in handle_pct since the first snapshot
- change since the previous snapshot
- snapshot age at the consumer's as-of time

Game-level summaries:

- majority-bet side for moneyline, spread, and total;
- majority-handle side for each market;
- bet/handle disagreement;
- largest absolute handle-versus-bet divergence;
- whether the public majority flipped between first and final snapshots;
- growth in num_picks and total_wagered;
- first-to-final movement for every side.

"Book side" is only shorthand for the side below 50% of bets. It must not be
presented as proof of sportsbook liability or a profitable sharp position.


Recommended downstream rollout

Phase 1: collection and observability

- Populate the Sheet without changing any expert or God Expert decision.
- Add collection health reporting: games expected, games bound, captures due,
  captures appended, final snapshots completed, and failures.
- Inspect at least one full NFL week for missing games, late event publication,
  percentage stability, and final-capture reliability.

Phase 2: read-only presentation

- Generate and display the shadow Pikkit Expert described below.
- Show the latest available split snapshot and its age in the Pikkit opinion.
- Show first versus final splits after kickoff for human analysis.
- Keep the figures explicitly labeled as Pikkit community data.

Phase 3: persisted MOE context

- Keep the Pikkit Expert's complete as-of input hash-bound to its opinion.
- Do not add raw Pikkit data to unrelated expert inputs during the shadow
  phase.
- Hash the selected Pikkit snapshot into the opinion input like every other
  generation-time artifact.
- Include the snapshot id, capture time, data age, market breakdowns, and
  derived divergence fields.
- Preserve byte-compatible behavior for legacy inputs that have no Pikkit
  block.

Phase 4: evaluation before influence

- Grade historical associations between Pikkit features and game outcomes,
  closing-line value, and existing expert/God decisions.
- Separate descriptive reporting from predictive claims.
- Do not change voice weights, the shared aggregator policy, edge thresholds,
  or stake sizing until enough pregame snapshots and resolved games exist to
  evaluate an explicitly proposed rule.
- Evaluation produces an activation recommendation only. The registry remains
  in shadow mode until the operator explicitly approves changing it.


Dedicated Pikkit Expert

Registry identity

- expert_id: pikkit
- name: Pikkit Expert
- input_profile: pikkit_splits
- mode: agent
- default_model: claude-opus-4-8
- reasoning_effort: max
- allowed_models: [claude-opus-4-8]
- committee_optional: true
- markets: [side, total]
- review: validation
- aggregator_participation: shadow

The existing committee_optional flag prevents an absent voice from blocking a
God run, but it does not stop an available optional voice from entering the
pools. Shadow mode therefore needs an explicit registry field enforced by
voice selection or feature construction. The row remains a normal approved
MOE opinion for display and grading; only aggregator participation is disabled.
Activation should be a registry-only change after the evaluation gate, with
the participation state copied into every subsequent aggregator input.

Input contract

The Pikkit Expert input should contain only information available at generation
time:

- game identity, teams, season, week, and kickoff;
- the first Pikkit snapshot;
- the latest snapshot at or before generation;
- the final T-2h snapshot when generation occurs at or after that capture;
- deterministic first-to-latest and previous-to-latest changes;
- bet/handle disagreement and divergence fields;
- snapshot ages and community sample counts;
- separately labeled BetOnline opening/latest market context when needed to
  identify favorite, underdog, posted total, and the market-implied baseline;
- deterministic sportsbook-benefit scenarios for moneyline, spread, and total;
- deterministic historical calibration from previously captured and resolved
  NFL games, using only games resolved before the current kickoff.

The input must state that Pikkit percentages are not tied to the displayed
BetOnline spread or total number. It must not contain later Pikkit snapshots,
post-kickoff lines, finals, grades, or later opinions.

Prediction contract

The deterministic input establishes the starting forecast from BetOnline:

- pairwise de-vigged home/away moneyline probabilities;
- spread-implied expected home margin;
- posted total as the starting projected total;
- the corresponding market-implied representative score.

Opus then decides whether the Pikkit evidence and historical calibration justify
moving away from that baseline. Every adjustment must identify:

- the baseline value;
- the deterministic Pikkit or calibration fields used;
- the direction and size of the adjustment;
- why the adjustment is warranted rather than merely describing public action;
- evidence that would argue against the adjustment.

No supplied signal means the output should remain near the market baseline,
not invent an edge. The model must not assume that a sportsbook-benefiting
outcome is destined to occur, but the estimated exposure is an allowed and
central predictive lens.


Deterministic sportsbook-benefit calculation

For each Pikkit snapshot, pair it with the latest BetOnline snapshot captured at
or before the Pikkit capture. Never use a later line or price.

For a two-sided market with handle shares h_a and h_b and representative
decimal prices d_a and d_b, estimate the book's net per unit of that market's
handle:

- outcome A wins: h_b - h_a * (d_a - 1)
- outcome B wins: h_a - h_b * (d_b - 1)
- push: 0, when the representative spread or total lands exactly

Returned winning stake is neutral in this net convention; the expression is
losing stakes retained minus winner profit paid. Report:

- normalized net for every possible market outcome;
- the highest-net and lowest-net outcome;
- the net gap between those outcomes;
- the lower-handle outcome as a separate line-free proxy;
- whether the lower-handle proxy and priced-net estimate agree;
- the assumptions and missing-data flags.

Moneyline outcomes are home win and away win. Spread outcomes are home cover,
away cover, and push at the representative BetOnline spread. Total outcomes are
over, under, and push at the representative BetOnline total.

Do not aggregate moneyline, spread, and total into one game-level dollar
liability. Pikkit does not provide handle allocation between markets or
cross-market bettor correlation. Each market remains a normalized independent
scenario.


Deterministic Pikkit statistics

Current-snapshot fields:

- bet and handle percentages for both sides;
- bet counts and total market bet count;
- handle-minus-bet percentage for each side;
- average-ticket index: handle share divided by bet share;
- majority-bet and majority-handle side;
- agreement or disagreement between those majorities;
- BetOnline market-implied probability, margin, total, and representative score;
- estimated sportsbook-net outcome table and exposure gap.

Movement fields:

- first, previous, and selected snapshot values;
- absolute and percentage-point changes in bet and handle shares;
- changes in average-ticket index and exposure gap;
- majority flips;
- divergence widening, narrowing, and zero-crossing;
- growth in bets, num_picks, and total_wagered;
- elapsed time and snapshot age.

Historical calibration fields, accumulated only from captured NFL data:

- result of each market's estimated sportsbook-preferred outcome;
- result of the lower-handle proxy outcome;
- records by market, favorite/underdog or over/under side, majority bucket,
  handle-divergence bucket, exposure-gap bucket, volume bucket, and movement
  pattern;
- Brier and margin error of prior initial and final Pikkit Expert opinions;
- ATS/O-U record and CLV of prior final shadow legs;
- market baseline performance over the same paired games;
- sample size and explicit insufficient-sample flags for every cohort.

Historical calculations must use only games whose kickoff and resolution
precede the current game's kickoff. Empty or undersized cohorts remain visible
as no-signal evidence rather than being omitted or generalized.

Initial analytical behavior

The voice should distinguish observations from claims:

- "public side" means more than 50% of recorded bets;
- "handle side" means more than 50% of recorded handle;
- handle-minus-bet divergence describes ticket-size concentration, not proven
  sharp action or sportsbook liability;
- a majority or divergence is not inherently contrarian or predictive;
- an estimated sportsbook-preferred outcome is not inherently destined to win;
- low sample counts, missing markets, stale snapshots, and unstable flips are
  explicit no-signal factors.

The expert must produce the normal MOE prediction fields so it can be graded in
shadow mode, but its rationale must identify when the supplied data offers no
demonstrated directional edge. Until historical calibration exists, confidence
should be constrained and the voice must not claim that a public or handle
pattern is profitable.

Output and leg policy

Both phases use an input-bound Pikkit-specific output schema with the normal MOE
prediction fields plus structured movement-watch results.

Initial phase:

- predicts winner, home-win probability, expected home margin, and final score;
- starts from the contemporaneous BetOnline market-implied forecast and records
  every adjustment;
- records movement watch conditions;
- forces side_pick_json and total_pick_json to PASS;
- states that no actionable leg is issued before movement is observed.

Final phase:

- evaluates every initial watch condition as triggered, not_triggered,
  invalidated, or unobservable;
- predicts winner, home-win probability, expected home margin, and final score;
- starts from the T-2h BetOnline market-implied forecast and records every
  adjustment;
- may issue a side leg, total leg, both, or PASS;
- records exactly which observed snapshot changes support each issued leg;
- remains a shadow result regardless of whether a leg fires.

The normalizer, not prompt wording alone, enforces initial-phase PASS legs and
requires the final response to account for every registered watch condition.

Generation and refresh

The collector persists snapshots only; it should not call a model inside the
Sheet write transaction. A separate generation step reads the committed rows,
builds a hash-bound input, generates the Pikkit opinion, and persists it through
the standard moe.generate_opinion rail.

Generation runner

Use a separate periodic runner rather than an explicit mutable queue. The
append-only snapshots and opinions are the durable work ledger.

For each upcoming or recently kicked-off game, the runner derives phase state:

- initial_due: a valid first snapshot exists and no valid initial opinion is
  linked to that first_snapshot_id;
- final_due: a valid final_t_minus_2h snapshot exists and no valid final opinion
  is linked to that snapshot plus the canonical initial opinion;
- complete: the required valid opinion already exists;
- data_incomplete: the phase snapshot exists but no market is complete;
- missed: the phase deadline passed without the required snapshot.

The runner must use deterministic phase identity so repeated scans cannot
duplicate successful work. An opinion identity is defined by expert id, NFL
event id, generation phase, selected snapshot id, and, for the final phase, the
initial opinion id. Invalid attempts remain audit rows but do not count as a
successful phase opinion.

Failure behavior:

- a Sheet read or validation failure aborts that game without changing data;
- an Opus transport failure appends no success-shaped row and remains retryable;
- an invalid model response follows the standard invalid-row and bounded-repair
  rail;
- a successful opinion append is terminal for that exact phase identity;
- one game's failure does not prevent other due games from running;
- the runner reports due, generated, already complete, incomplete, missed, and
  failed counts.

The initial runner may retry until the final phase becomes due. The final runner
may retry only while the game remains at least one hour from kickoff, matching
the God Judge cutoff and leaving time for operator visibility. A final failure
inside that boundary remains visible as missed rather than generating from a
later or postgame state.

Generation should be sequential by kickoff time to control subscription usage
and keep logs attributable. Each Opus call records model, effort, phase, input
hash, output status, token/usage metadata when available, and wall time in a
dedicated JSONL runs ledger.

Two generation phases are required:

1. Initial: run immediately after the first successful Pikkit snapshot. It
   describes the opening bettor behavior and pre-registers observable movement
   conditions to check later.
2. Final: run immediately after the mandatory T-2h snapshot. It receives the
   first snapshot, every intervening 12-hour snapshot, the final snapshot, and
   the hash-bound initial opinion. It reports which watch conditions occurred,
   which did not occur, and how the evidence changed its final prediction.

The initial opinion must not use generic hindsight-friendly language. Its
movement watch should be structured and testable. Each watch item should carry:

- market: moneyline | spread | total;
- side: home | away | over | under;
- metric: bet_pct | handle_pct | handle_minus_bet_pct | majority_side;
- expected movement: rise | fall | widen | narrow | flip | remain stable;
- threshold or an explicit explanation that only a directional observation is
  intended;
- interpretation if observed;
- interpretation if not observed;
- conditions that would invalidate the interpretation.

Examples of observable patterns the initial voice may register, without
claiming they are inherently predictive:

- a bet-majority flip;
- a handle-majority flip;
- handle concentration increasing while bet share remains stable;
- bet/handle divergence widening, narrowing, or crossing zero;
- one side gaining most new bets but not most new handle;
- agreement or disagreement emerging across moneyline and spread;
- total behavior diverging from side-market behavior;
- a signal disappearing as num_picks grows.

The final opinion may discuss only changes supported by the stored snapshots.
It must distinguish a triggered watch condition from a profitable betting
signal and must explicitly note low growth, stale observations, or missing
markets.

Sparse and missing data

A market is complete when both opposing sides are present, their percentages
are valid, and their paired percentages approximately sum to 1.0. Initial and
final opinions may be generated when at least one of moneyline, spread, or total
is complete.

There is no initial hard cutoff on num_picks or market bet counts. The input
must carry those counts, and the opinion must call out limited participation
when appropriate, but shadow collection should preserve the model's behavior
across the observed volume distribution rather than discard low-volume cases.

Leg eligibility is market-specific:

- an incomplete spread blocks a side spread leg;
- an incomplete total blocks a total leg;
- missing moneyline does not block spread or total analysis;
- if no market is complete, no opinion is generated and the runner records a
  data-incomplete status for retry before the phase deadline.

Volume buckets and any future minimum threshold are derived from observed data
and evaluated later. They are not chosen before the first collection period.

The final opinion becomes the default Pikkit Expert row shown in the standard
game summary. The initial opinion remains available in detail/history so a
reader can compare the pre-registered watch with the final assessment.

Opinion phase and grading identity

The two phases must not be counted as two independent games in the main expert
scoreboard. Persist an explicit phase identity, at minimum:

- generation_phase: initial | final_t_minus_2h
- prior_opinion_id: blank for initial; initial opinion id for final
- first_snapshot_id
- selected_snapshot_id
- snapshot_ids used by the final opinion

These fields may live in a dedicated structured Pikkit summary if changing the
common opinion columns is unnecessary, but readers and graders must validate
them rather than infer phase from timestamps.

The normal Pikkit Expert scoreboard uses the final phase only. A separate
research comparison grades initial and final phases side by side:

- initial versus final Brier and margin error;
- prediction and confidence changes;
- whether each registered watch condition triggered;
- whether triggered conditions improved the final forecast;
- whether final movement added value over the initial forecast and market
  baseline.

Because shadow opinions do not enter the aggregator, neither phase can change a
God committee key or trigger a judge call during the observation phase.

Shadow evaluation

Track the Pikkit Expert like every other voice:

- Brier score for home-win probability;
- predicted-margin error;
- ATS and O/U result at close;
- fired-leg record and CLV if the voice schema permits legs;
- calibration by bet-majority side, handle-majority side, divergence bucket,
  market, sample-size bucket, and first-to-final movement;
- comparison with the market baseline and the existing committee.

Any activation review must evaluate whether the voice adds information beyond
the market and existing voices, not merely whether its raw win rate is above
50%. Passing such a review does not activate the voice; activation is a manual
operator decision made between NFL weeks.


Desk presentation

Shared Picks card:

- show one Pikkit Expert line in the normal voice order;
- before the final opinion, label it Initial and show prediction, probability,
  projected score, and that both legs are PASS;
- after the final opinion, replace the shared summary with the Final T-2h
  prediction and any shadow legs;
- include one compact movement phrase naming the strongest observed,
  non-misleading change, for example handle divergence widened or bet majority
  flipped;
- include the compact deterministic sportsbook-preferred outcomes separately
  from the Opus prediction so readers can distinguish calculation from model
  judgment;
- keep detailed watch conditions inside the expandable Why content.

Offline card:

- retain the latest compact Pikkit line available when the card freezes;
- include the phase and selected snapshot time so cached content is
  self-describing.

DM full-opinion view:

- show the initial opinion and its registered movement watch;
- show the final opinion and every watch item's outcome;
- show first, final, and net changes for all complete markets;
- link the final opinion to the initial opinion id and both selected snapshots;
- make shadow status explicit so no displayed leg is mistaken for an active God
  or production recommendation.
